Honey bees (Apis mellifera) are social insects that live in organized colonies.
A colony usually contains one queen, many worker bees, and seasonally some drones.
The queen's main role is egg laying, while worker bees perform tasks such as nursing, cleaning, guarding, and foraging.

Worker bees collect nectar and pollen from flowers.
Nectar is converted into honey through enzymatic activity and evaporation inside the hive.
Pollen is an important protein source for developing larvae.
Bees store honey in wax cells and cap it when moisture is low enough.

Honey bees communicate food locations using the waggle dance.
The angle of the dance indicates direction relative to the sun, and the duration indicates distance.
This communication helps the colony forage efficiently.

Bees are major pollinators in natural ecosystems and agriculture.
Pollination by bees increases yields of many crops, including apples, almonds, berries, and cucumbers.
A decline in pollinators can reduce crop quality and quantity.

Bees face threats such as habitat loss, pesticides, parasites, and diseases.
The Varroa destructor mite is one of the most damaging parasites of honey bee colonies.
Integrated pest management and careful hive monitoring help reduce Varroa impact.

Swarming is a natural reproductive process of colonies.
Before swarming, the old queen leaves with part of the worker population to establish a new nest.
The original colony raises a new queen from specially fed larvae.

Beekeepers support colonies by providing hive space, monitoring health, and ensuring food availability during scarce periods.
In winter, colonies cluster to maintain temperature around the brood area.
Good ventilation and moisture control are important in cold seasons.

Royal jelly is a nutrient-rich secretion fed to all larvae early in life and continuously to queen larvae.
The queen develops differently from workers due to diet and epigenetic effects.

Bees can sting in defense of the colony.
Honey bee workers have barbed stingers and usually die after stinging mammals.
Calm handling, protective gear, and smoke are commonly used in beekeeping to reduce defensive behavior.
